03 · Case studies

Running the numbers for a five-room hospitality business

What a finance function looks like when it is one person
Five rooms Rural Spain Direct booking channel
Challenge
A five-room hospitality business in rural Spain, full through the summer and thin for the rest of the year, with every booking arriving through a third-party platform. The platform charges the owner a commission, and charges the guest a separate fee on top of the nightly price.
Approach
Built a direct booking and payment channel on the owner's own website, and modelled the rate structure behind it: seasonal tiers, two-night minimum stays, and bundles for booking further ahead.
Where it stands
That rate structure is what the business uses today, and I produce the reporting the owner runs the season on. The forward plan and a cheaper payment provider are still in progress.
The part I find most interesting

Because the platform's guest fee disappears on a direct booking, the same room can be cheaper to the guest at no cost to the owner. The pricing room was already there; it just needed somewhere to go.

What each sales channel actually costs

A cost model over an 11,000-row dataset
11,000 rows
The question
I built a model over an 11,000-row dataset to answer a question that sounds simple and is not: which sales channels actually make money once every cost of serving them is counted, not just the obvious ones.
What the model does
It allocates costs out to each channel rather than leaving them in one blended pile, separates the costs that are fixed by how the business is set up from the ones a manager can change month to month, tracks performance against targets, projects a run rate forward, and flags data that looks wrong before it reaches the output.
The part I would defend hardest

The part I would defend hardest is the last one. A model nobody trusts gets ignored, and the fastest way to lose trust is one wrong number that someone spots before you do.

Reconciliation automation

Goldman Sachs Asset Management
−50% processing time ~12 breaks surfaced Python + SQL
Challenge
Daily fund-price reconciliations were manual, repetitive, and slow. That was time the team could have spent on actual exceptions.
Approach
Proved the concept in Excel first, then rebuilt it properly: SQL pulls joining data on ISIN and fund code, with Python flagging breaks against basis-point tolerances.
Result
The proof of concept alone halved the processing time; the automation surfaced around a dozen pricing breaks that manual checks could miss, and I wrote the manual so it outlived my tenure.
The part I actually find interesting

It wasn't complicated, and that's the point. The value wasn't in clever code; it was in noticing that a process everyone accepted as manual didn't have to be, proving it cheaply in a tool everyone already trusted (Excel), and only then investing in the production version. Sequencing beats sophistication.

Capital structure & corporate investment

MSc thesis · Rotterdam School of Management
2,355 firms 2014–2024 panel Graded 8.5/10
Question
How does capital structure influence corporate investment decisions?
Approach
Econometric panel analysis of 2,355 listed European firms across 2014–2024, estimated with GMM to untangle the two-way causality between financing and investment.
Findings
Higher book leverage materially reduces CapEx (a debt-overhang effect); R&D is driven more by cash-flow volatility than by financing mix, and equity issuance alone doesn't lift it.
Why it matters outside academia

Debt overhang is not an abstraction: it's the reason a leveraged portfolio company underinvests right when it should be growing. Reading 2,355 balance sheets against ten years of investment decisions changes how you look at any single one.
